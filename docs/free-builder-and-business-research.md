# Free builder and business research

HorizonX's public catalog describes a preview → inspect source → add to project workflow, alongside component kits, icons, assets, and shaders. Its server requires a subscription. FRIDAY uses free source references from shadcn and Watermelon instead; HorizonX was removed from the Codex configuration. No subscriber components were accessed.

In Work, open **Builder**, select a stack and blocks, inspect the starter previews/source, and prepare an implementation. The saved kit, business listing, and latest verified details become selected project context. The request is reviewed before sending it to the configured model. Kits are starting structures with visible placeholders; generating and validating a completed website still requires a model and a local folder.

Available recipes are portable HTML, React/TypeScript/Vite, and Dioramas. Dioramas contributes its inspectable engine API, scene review workflow, and procedural/supplied asset approach. Image generation, fal/Meshy pipelines, and paid services are excluded. The 3D dependencies belong to the generated website rather than the desktop application. Upstream code is MIT; upstream generated assets have separate attribution requirements, so supply your own assets or use procedural geometry.

Portable sites preview from index.html. Module-based sites can use a separate loopback development URL; the iframe retains its opaque sandbox and never receives allow-same-origin. A generated Vite server must allow the literal `null` CORS origin for module loading in that frame and remain bound to loopback. Open the local app externally to review storage-dependent behavior. URL checks reject remote servers, credentials, script schemes, and FRIDAY's origin.

The map supports business/category, website-listing, and contact filters. Business details combine exact OSM records, the exact linked Wikidata entity's phone/website claims, and contacts extracted from public-site links and structured data. **Google discovery** opens a visible Search/Maps window without a paid API key. Browse that window, return to FRIDAY, and choose **Import visible results** to bring in the loaded result links, snippets, phone/address candidates, and quoted text. Review a result's business name and save it to **Website leads**, then use **Research details** and **Build a website for this business**. Google research leads keep their source and excerpt; they have no map pin or directions until business coordinates are known. Public sources can omit facts or block requests; absence is kept visible and does not prove a business has no website.

Research candidates require business-identity confirmation before saving. Changed fields retain their source URL, provider, and verification time; unchanged fields preserve their existing provenance. The original project listing remains intact, with a separate latest-details capture for updates. Read-only model tools `business.search` and `business.read` expose the same free research path. Native reads validate public addresses across redirects, bound request time and response size, and execute no site scripts.

For a selected business, **Get Google business details** opens its Maps listing and automatically captures a matching business. For duplicate names, choose the correct branch in Google Maps and press **Capture selected listing** in FRIDAY; that button stays available while research runs. After capture the Google window closes and the same search panel expands into a dossier with source-linked photos, contact, location, hours/status, website, and a website-project action. Photos have individual ASCII loading states and source-viewer fallback; fields resolve through ASCII plates. Confirm the branch before saving or building. **Cancel research** / **Close Google window** handle interrupted research. Escape or the close control collapses the dossier while retaining its captured details. Business headings use Manrope; secondary research/actions/source evidence live in compact disclosures. See ADR 021 for matching and lifecycle limits.

Navigation keeps the previously loaded workspace visible while the requested route opens, with a compact ASCII status. Data refreshes preserve their existing content rather than replacing it with rows of placeholder glyphs; newly loaded blocks decode into place. Both OS and FRIDAY reduced-motion preferences stop repeated glyph changes. Memory and provider settings use bounded working panes. Shared onboarding covers Google discovery and the current builder features.
